From Crime Scenes to Clean Scenes: Christina Hester Featured on Women in Power TV

CSI: Clean Scene Investigators founder Christina Hester shares the story behind her path from a homicide victim’s family member, to Crime Scene Investigator, to leading a Dallas-Fort Worth crime scene cleanup company.

Published September 14, 2026 · CSI: Clean Scene Investigators · Dallas-Fort Worth, Texas

Some stories are hard to tell because there is no clean beginning.

For Christina Hester, the story behind CSI: Clean Scene Investigators started long before there was a company.

It started with loss.

It continued behind crime scene tape.

And eventually, it became a mission to help families through the moments nobody prepares them for.

Christina Hester, Founder and CEO of CSI: Clean Scene Investigators, is officially featured on Women in Power TV by Inside Success.

A perspective built on every side of the crime scene tape

Christina's connection to this work is different from most.

She has experienced violent crime from the perspective of a victim's family.

She later became a Crime Scene Investigator, documenting scenes, processing evidence and seeing firsthand what happens during some of the worst moments of people's lives.

Today, she leads CSI: Clean Scene Investigators, providing professional crime scene cleanup, trauma cleanup, blood cleanup, unattended death and decomposition remediation, forensic odor removal, hazardous hoarding remediation and vehicle biohazard cleanup throughout the Dallas-Fort Worth Metroplex and North Texas.

That combination gives Christina a perspective that cannot be learned from a training manual.

She understands what happens before, during and after the crime scene tape comes down.

What happens after police leave a crime scene?

One of the biggest misconceptions families encounter is believing that law enforcement cleans the property after an investigation.

They generally do not.

Once a crime scene is released, the responsibility for arranging cleanup typically falls to the property owner, family, landlord, property manager or another authorized person.

That is where professional crime scene and biohazard remediation companies like CSI step in.

CSI helps families and property professionals across DFW and North Texas handle the physical aftermath of scenes involving blood, bodily fluids, decomposition, unattended deaths, homicide, suicide and other traumatic events.

The work is technical. But it is also deeply human.

Women in Power

Christina's Women in Power episode shares more than the story of building a company.

It is about what happens when tragedy, forensic experience and purpose collide.

It is about taking the things that could have ended your story and using them to build something that helps somebody else survive theirs.

And it is about redefining what power actually looks like.

For Christina, power is not pretending the hard things never happened.

It is deciding what you are going to do with them.
