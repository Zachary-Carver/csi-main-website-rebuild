Immediate guidance after a traumatic event

You do not have to know every next step right now.

After a death, crime, medical emergency, assault, accident, or other traumatic event, people are often expected to make decisions while shocked, grieving, exhausted, or overwhelmed.

Begin with immediate safety. Allow emergency responders and investigators to complete their work. After the scene is released, CSI can help explain the physical cleanup process, affected materials, property access, documentation, payment, and appropriate next steps.

Pause. Breathe. Prioritize safety.

The entire situation does not have to be solved in one moment.

Focus first on immediate danger, emergency response, scene release, safe housing, authorized contacts, and preventing unnecessary exposure.

Emergency and crisis support

Contact emergency services before contacting a cleanup company.

Call 911 when someone may be injured, in immediate danger, missing, unresponsive, or when a crime, fire, hazardous condition, or medical emergency may still be active.

The first four priorities

Address safety before property cleanup.

01

02

Contact Authorities

Allow emergency services, law enforcement, medical personnel, fire personnel, or other appropriate authorities to respond.

03

Wait for Scene Release

Do not enter, move property, clean surfaces, or disturb the scene until the appropriate authority confirms it has been released.

04

Identify the Authorized Contact

Determine who may approve property access, cleanup services, personal-property decisions, documentation, and payment.

What not to do

Avoid actions that could create additional exposure or interfere with the scene.

Families, employees, landlords, tenants, and maintenance personnel should not be expected to perform traumatic or biologically hazardous cleanup without the appropriate systems and equipment.

  • Do not enter an active or unreleased scene
  • Do not touch blood, bodily fluids, tissues, or unknown substances
  • Do not pick up needles, blades, glass, or unidentified sharp objects
  • Do not wash contaminated bedding or clothing in a household machine
  • Do not use a household vacuum on affected materials
  • Do not rely on bleach alone to resolve hidden contamination
  • Do not remove flooring, walls, furniture, or belongings prematurely
  • Do not post photographs, addresses, or private scene details online
  • Do not allow children, pets, tenants, or visitors into the area
  • Do not promise insurance coverage or reimbursement

Possible exposure

Seek medical guidance promptly after contact with blood, bodily fluids, or a sharp object.

Wash affected skin with soap and water. Flush the eyes, nose, or mouth with water after a splash. Do not squeeze, cut, scrub aggressively, or apply harsh chemicals to a puncture or exposure site.

Contact a medical professional, emergency department, occupational-health provider, or other appropriate healthcare resource for individualized evaluation. Cleanup contractors do not diagnose infections or replace medical care.

Do not make irreversible decisions while still in shock.

The emotional aftermath

Trauma can affect memory, concentration, sleep, decision-making, and the ability to process information.

Ask a trusted person to take notes, help manage telephone calls, organize documents, care for children or animals, arrange transportation, and sit with you while decisions are made.

CSI is not emergency medical services, law enforcement, a crisis center, a mental-health provider, an attorney, an insurance company, a public adjuster, or a funeral home.

This page provides general guidance and cannot determine whether a scene is safe, released, legally accessible, medically urgent, covered by insurance, or appropriate for a specific service.

Immediate guidance questions

Frequently asked questions after a traumatic event.

Who should I call first?

Contact 911 first when someone may be injured, in immediate danger, unresponsive, missing, or when a crime, fire, medical emergency, or hazardous condition may still be active.

When can the cleanup company enter?

CSI should enter only after the appropriate authority releases the scene and an authorized property, estate, business, or vehicle contact grants access.

Should I start cleaning while I wait?

No. Avoid touching, washing, moving, vacuuming, transporting, or removing affected materials until the scene is released and you receive appropriate guidance.

What should I do after contact with blood or bodily fluids?

Wash affected skin with soap and water, flush splashes with water, and seek prompt medical guidance. Contact emergency or occupational medical care after a needlestick, sharp injury, or significant exposure.

Will law enforcement clean the property?

Emergency personnel and law enforcement manage the emergency or investigation. Property cleanup is generally coordinated separately by the authorized owner, family, estate, business, or property contact.

Should I photograph the scene?

Do not enter an unsafe or unreleased scene to take photographs. Ask the investigating authority, insurer, attorney, or cleanup provider what documentation is appropriate.

Can insurance help pay for cleanup?

Some policies may provide benefits depending on the event, policy language, exclusions, deductible, authorization, documentation, and insurer decision. Coverage is not guaranteed.

What information does CSI need from me?

Begin with the city, property type, broad situation, affected area, scene-release status, authorized contact, safe contact information, and preferred timing.
