Why professional remediation matters

What is visible may not be the full extent of the scene.

Biological contamination may reach seams, subfloors, wall cavities, upholstery, padding, and other porous materials. The affected environment must be evaluated rather than treated as a routine surface-cleaning project.

01

Hidden Migration

Fluids can move beneath flooring, behind trim, into furniture, and through gaps that are easy to overlook.

02

Porous Materials

Carpet, padding, drywall, unfinished wood, insulation, and textiles may not respond like hard surfaces.

03

Cross-Contamination

Improper movement through the space can spread contamination to tools, shoes, adjacent rooms, and contents.

04

Odor at the Source

Lasting odor concerns require identifying and addressing the affected source materials rather than masking them.

The CSI remediation process

Careful from the first call through the final walkthrough.

Every scene is different. These steps represent the general process used to evaluate and address a crime-scene cleanup.

01

Confidential Contact

We gather essential information, confirm that the scene is available for cleanup, and discuss access and timing.

02

Scene Assessment

The affected area, materials, potential migration, odor, safety concerns, and property conditions are evaluated.

03

Containment and Removal

The work area is controlled and materials that cannot be appropriately remediated are identified and addressed.

04

Cleaning and Disinfection

Affected surfaces and remaining materials are cleaned, treated, and deodorized according to the scene conditions.

05

Final Communication

We explain the completed scope and identify any remaining reconstruction, replacement, or property considerations.

What the scope may include

A plan based on the actual environment.

The services required depend on what happened, how long the area was affected, and which materials were involved.

  • Blood and bodily-fluid remediation
  • Contaminated-content assessment
  • Carpet and padding evaluation
  • Subfloor and structural-surface assessment
  • Cleaning and disinfection of affected surfaces
  • Removal of non-remediable materials when necessary
  • Odor-source identification and treatment
  • Biohazard waste packaging and transport coordination
  • Vehicle interior assessment and remediation
  • Clear communication with authorized property contacts

Frequently asked questions

What families and property owners often ask.

When can crime scene cleanup begin?

Cleanup can generally begin after the appropriate authority has released the affected area and authorized access is available. Do not enter or disturb an active or unreleased scene.

Should I clean anything before CSI arrives?

No. Avoid touching, moving, or attempting to clean affected materials. Limiting access can reduce unnecessary contact and prevent contamination from being moved elsewhere.

How long does crime scene cleanup take?

Timing depends on the size of the affected area, materials involved, degree of contamination, access conditions, and whether structural materials require removal.

Can CSI clean homes, businesses, and rentals?

Yes. CSI works with families, property owners, landlords, businesses, hotels, property managers, rental operators, and other authorized contacts.

Do you serve all of DFW?

CSI serves the Dallas–Fort Worth Metroplex and surrounding North Texas communities. Contact us to confirm availability for your city and situation.

Can you also address odor and decomposition?

Yes. When decomposition or persistent odor is involved, the scope may include source-material assessment, material removal, cleaning, disinfection, and specialized odor treatment.

Will my inquiry be treated confidentially?

CSI treats service inquiries and scene information with discretion. Share only the information needed for us to understand the situation and determine the next step.
